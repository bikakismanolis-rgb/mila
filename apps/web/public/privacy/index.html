Privacy policy

Last updated: 19 October 2026

Mila is a messenger that uses your email address instead of your phone number. This page explains what we hold, why we hold it, and how you get rid of it. It is written to be read, not to be survived.

Your messages and calls are end-to-end encrypted. Text, photos, videos, voice messages and files are locked on your device, with a key that never leaves it, before they are sent. Our servers store only the locked version and cannot open it. The sound and picture of a call go directly between the devices in the call, encrypted; they never pass through our servers in a form anyone can hear or see. What we can see is who talks to whom and when, who called whom and for how long, how large an attachment is, group names, reactions, and your profile.

What we collect

Your email address How you sign in, and how other people find you. Required. Other users see it only after you have accepted a conversation with them, and you can hide it from everyone in settings. Searching by part of an address is not possible; someone has to type the whole address.
Your password Stored only as a cryptographic hash by our authentication provider. We never see it. Not collected if you sign in with Google.
Display name, username, bio, photo Whatever you choose to put on your profile. All optional except the name. Strangers who find you by your full email see only your name. Your photo and bio are visible only to people you chat with. Before a photo is uploaded, your device redraws it at a small size, which strips its location and camera details.
Your messages and attachments The encrypted form of what you send, which we cannot read, plus who sent it, to which conversation, when, and the size of any file. Also the reactions people add and which group conversations you are in. Stored until the sender deletes a message “for everyone”, which erases it and its files from our servers. “Delete for me” only hides a message from your own view. Messages sent before encryption was introduced (October 2026) are stored as plain text until their sender deletes them; Settings offers a button for that.
Calls Who called whom, in which conversation, whether it was a voice or video call, when it started, who answered, and when it ended. This is what makes the phone ring, shows missed calls, and fills your call history. We never receive or record what is said or shown: the audio and video are encrypted between the devices. While a call is running your device tells us every few seconds that it is still in the call, so that a call whose app was closed abruptly ends on its own; those signals are deleted when the call ends.
Online status and last seen When you last had Mila open. Shown only to people you share an accepted conversation with, and only if you also show yours. You can turn it off in settings, in which case you don’t see anyone else’s either.
Chat settings Which conversations you muted, pinned or archived. Visible only to you; the other people in the conversation are not told.
Device keys The public half of the encryption key of each device you use, a short label such as “Firefox · Windows”, and when it was last used. Public keys are what other people’s devices need to lock messages for you. The private half never leaves your device.
Delivery and read receipts When a message reached someone’s device and when they opened it. You can turn read receipts off in settings; delivery (two grey ticks) is always shown, as in other messengers.
Contact matching (only if you ask for it) If you upload a contact list to find people you know, your device hashes the email addresses before sending them. We never receive the addresses themselves and we do not store the hashes after matching.
Notification address (only if you turn notifications on) A delivery address for each device, issued by your browser’s push service, plus the keys needed to encrypt notifications for that device. Removed when you turn notifications off, sign out, or delete your account.
Blocks and reports Who you blocked, and any abuse reports you filed or that named you.
Invitations: nothing When you invite a friend, the phone number or email you type stays on your device. We never receive it and it is not stored anywhere, not on our servers and not in your browser. Mila only opens your own SMS, WhatsApp or email app with a ready-made message, and you decide whether to send it from there.
Usage counters A record that your account performed an action (sent a message, searched for an email, started a conversation) and when, without its content. Used only to enforce limits that stop spam and stop anyone from checking lists of emails. Deleted after 31 days.

We never collect phone numbers, yours or your friends', your contacts list, your location, or your browsing activity, and no device identifiers beyond the notification address described above. There is no advertising in Mila and no analytics or tracking code on this page.

Why we are allowed to hold it

  • To provide the service you asked for (GDPR Article 6(1)(b)) — your account, your profile, your conversations. Without this there is no app.
  • Our legitimate interest in keeping the service safe (Article 6(1)(f)) — blocks, abuse reports, and rate limiting exist so that people can use Mila without being harassed.
  • Your consent (Article 6(1)(a)) — contact matching only runs when you explicitly start it, and you can withdraw at any time by not running it again.

Who else touches your data

We are a small operation and we do not run our own infrastructure. These companies process data on our behalf, under contract, and are not allowed to use it for their own purposes.

Supabase Database, authentication and file storage. Your account, profile, messages and attachments live here. Region: Frankfurt, Germany (EU).
Vercel Serves the web app. Sees your IP address as any web server would.
Resend Sends account confirmation and password reset emails. Receives your email address.
Google Only if you choose Google Sign-In. Google confirms your identity to us; we receive your email address and name.
Call connection servers (Google, Cloudflare) When a call starts, each device asks a public “STUN” server run by Google or Cloudflare for its own internet address, so the two devices can find each other. That server sees your IP address and nothing else. When two networks don’t allow a direct connection, the call is relayed through Cloudflare’s TURN service. The relay passes along data that is already encrypted end to end, so it sees IP addresses and how much data flows, never the sound or picture.
Your browser’s push service (Google, Apple, Mozilla or Microsoft) Only if you turn notifications on. Notifications travel through the service built into your browser or phone. The content is encrypted for your device before it leaves us, so the service can see that a notification was sent and when, but not what it says. Notifications never contain the text of a message: we cannot read it either. A call notification says only who is calling and whether it is a video call.

How long we keep it

Your account and profile stay until you delete them. Messages stay until the person who sent them deletes them for everyone. Abuse reports are kept after account deletion, without identifying details, because a safety record that vanishes when the reported person leaves is not a safety record.

Deleting your account

Settings → Your account → Delete my account. It takes effect immediately and cannot be undone.

What is erased

  • Your login, password and Google connection.
  • Your email address — it is released and someone else could register it.
  • Your display name, username, bio and profile photo.
  • Your block list, your reactions, your chat settings, and your notification addresses.
  • Your membership of any group conversations.

What is not

Messages you already sent stay in the recipient's conversation, shown as coming from a deleted user with no name or address attached. We keep them because that conversation is also the other person's record, and deleting one side of it would take away something that is theirs as much as yours.

If you want your sent messages destroyed as well, write to us and say so. That is your right under GDPR Article 17 and we will do it by hand.

Your rights

If you are in the EU or the UK, the GDPR gives you the following. You can exercise the first two yourself, in the app, right now.

  • Access and portability — Settings → Your account → Download my data gives you everything we hold about you as a JSON file.
  • Erasure — the delete button above, or write to us for the harder version.
  • Rectification — edit your profile, or ask us.
  • Objection and restriction — you can object to processing based on our legitimate interest.
  • Complaint — you can complain to your national data protection authority. In Greece this is the Hellenic Data Protection Authority (dpa.gr). You do not need to go through us first.

Children

Mila is not for anyone under 16. We do not knowingly hold data about children. If you believe a child has an account, tell us and we will remove it.

Security

Traffic is encrypted in transit. Messages and attachments are also end-to-end encrypted, so even with full access to our database they cannot be read. Access to the database is governed by row-level rules so that one account cannot read another's conversations, and every action is rate limited. Passwords are hashed.

The limits, plainly: our encryption does not yet have forward secrecy (if the key on one of your devices is stolen, past messages sent to that device can be opened). A new device cannot read messages sent before it was set up. And because our servers hand out public keys, you should compare the safety code in a contact’s details with them in person if you need to be certain no one is in between.

Changes

If we change this policy in a way that matters, we will say so in the app before the change takes effect, not quietly edit this page. The date at the top always reflects the current version.
